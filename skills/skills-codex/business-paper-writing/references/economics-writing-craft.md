# Economics writing craft: optional Cochrane / McCloskey / Shapiro framework

Read this when the user requests this named writing tradition, an economics-style first draft, or an alternative manuscript structure. This is an adaptation of the full writing framework in Barrios's `econ-write`, derived from Lu Han's collection of writing guides. It covers manuscript structure, section craft, prose, exhibits, and revision; it is not a reproduction of the authors' books or a claim that each rule was written by all three authors.

The user chooses whether to apply this framework. For an existing manuscript, preserve its organization and voice unless structural revision is requested. Supplied journal requirements and the user's explicit style choices govern application. Use the relevant sections only; a named style does not require an author-sample file or a separate style-profile artifact.

## Reader and argument

- Write for an informed reader who does not already know the setting. Supply the institutional detail, construct definitions, and assumptions needed to understand the next claim.
- Make the research question, main answer, and economic contribution intelligible early. Develop detail after the reader knows why it matters.
- Organize around the central contribution. Additional results should explain, qualify, or extend that contribution.
- Present the final argument. A chronology of searches, failed specifications, or drafting decisions belongs in research records when needed.
- Prefer concrete subjects and ordinary verbs. Use technical language when it is needed for the construct, estimand, or mechanism.

## Title and abstract

Name the research object and core relationship, finding, or mechanism. A method belongs in the title when it is itself the contribution. Causal words require the design that supports them. Evaluate specificity and intelligibility rather than assigning a title score or a fixed word count.

An empirical abstract conveys the question, setting/design needed to interpret it, main finding with an appropriate magnitude, and incremental contribution. A theory abstract names the insight and mechanism; a structural abstract gives the counterfactual finding and defining assumptions. Check the target outlet's word limit. A provisional abstract can guide drafting; revise it after the argument and results are settled.

## Introduction

The upstream framework's triangular introduction is an available structure:

1. Establish the economic problem, documented fact, or puzzle.
2. State the concrete research question and the setting or mechanism that makes the answer possible.
3. Give the main finding or proposition and explain its economic meaning.
4. Position the contribution against the closest studies, describing the actual increment.
5. Include a short roadmap when it helps the reader navigate this paper.

These are argumentative functions, not paragraph quotas. Use the existing manuscript's order when it carries the argument well. Do not require three contributions, fixed page percentages, a roadmap, or a separate literature section solely to match a template. A paper's literature discussion can be integrated into the introduction or kept in an existing section.

Motivation names the economic stakes and the unresolved question. Contribution prose explains what the paper establishes relative to prior work. A real conflict between theories or findings can motivate the paper; do not invent a rejected alternative to make the contribution sound stronger.

## Literature and hypotheses

Group close studies by question, mechanism, measurement, design, and findings. Explain what the relevant evidence establishes and where the new paper changes the answer. Credit prior contributions accurately and distinguish published and working-paper versions. Select close studies by relevance rather than a fixed citation count.

For hypothesis development, state the economic channel and its prediction. Retain a competing prediction when it is an actual implication of the setting or theory. A hypothesis cannot be created merely because an available regression tests it. Route discovery and source verification to the existing literature and claim-audit skills when needed.

## Data and institutional setting

State data sources, coverage, period, and observation unit. Explain the sample restrictions, joins, measurement choices, and information dates that affect interpretation. Give final sample counts and material attrition from actual outputs. Include institutional facts needed to understand the source of variation; preserve their sources.

Describe treatment, outcomes, and important controls precisely. Define labels before using them. Explain restricted-data access and material coverage gaps in the appropriate data section. Keep extensive cleaning details in documentation or an appendix when they do not carry the argument.

## Research design and empirical results

Explain the source of identifying variation in economic terms before relying on an equation. Specify comparison groups, timing, estimand, and the assumptions defining the claim. Read [empirical reporting](empirical-reporting.md) for method-specific narration and units, and [empirical diagnostics](../../shared-references/business-empirical-diagnostics.md) for the selected design.

Lead the results discussion with the finding that answers the question. Report a meaningful contrast or coefficient with uncertainty and a supported economic benchmark. Explain changes across specifications through actual sample, measurement, and model differences. Coefficient stability by itself does not establish identification.

Keep null and mixed findings visible. Interpret intervals relative to economically meaningful values. Heterogeneity, robustness, and mechanism analyses belong where they answer substantive questions or resolve concrete threats. Identify exploratory analyses and consequential deviations from a supplied analysis plan when applicable.

## Theory, mixed, and structural papers

- **Theory:** introduce the economic environment, actors, information, timing, incentives, and constraints before formal results. Explain the intuition of propositions and distinguish essential from simplifying assumptions. A useful example can precede the general case. Place long proofs in an appendix when the main argument remains understandable.
- **Mixed theory and empirics:** connect tested predictions to the model's mechanisms or propositions. State which predictions are tested and which remain theoretical implications.
- **Structural:** distinguish economic assumptions, identifying variation, and functional-form restrictions. Explain estimation, validation/model fit, and the counterfactual experiment. Report the counterfactual's population, horizon, and welfare or outcome units.
- **Descriptive/measurement:** explain the new fact or measurement improvement, coverage and construct validity, and its economic contribution using the evidence-supported claim level.

Preserve the project's formalism and notation where they are necessary. Define symbols on first use and remind readers of distant definitions when needed. Introduce displayed equations verbally and number those referenced elsewhere.

## Sentences, paragraphs, and author voice

Use an identifiable subject and a verb that conveys the claim. Active voice often makes responsibility clear; passive voice remains useful when the actor is irrelevant. Keep the supplied authorial person and use it consistently. Tables and figures can serve as subjects when they provide the evidence.

Let paragraph openings carry the question, claim, or necessary definition. Develop one argumentative function per paragraph and connect paragraphs through their substance. Resolve ambiguous pronouns and long noun stacks. Choose sentence length from its informational job; do not impose a numerical rhythm or manufacture irregularity.

For a requested prose cleanup, read [academic prose editing](academic-prose-editing.md). Preserve negation, uncertainty, attribution, statistical qualification, and causal scope during shortening. Word substitutions must not recast the contribution or increase confidence.

## Tables, figures, and appendices

Make exhibits readable on their own: define sample, variables, units, specifications, uncertainty, fixed effects, and clustering as relevant. Give readers enough information to identify the model behind a regression column. Explain the key comparison in the text; do not narrate every cell.

Use figures for distributions, dynamics, nonlinearities, and patterns; use tables when exact values or specification comparisons carry the argument. Read [empirical figures](../../data-analysis-bridge/references/empirical-figures.md) when producing them. Preserve meaningful precision rather than rounding everything to a fixed number of decimals. Follow the journal's conventions for stars and notes.

Keep critical evidence in the main argument. Organize supplementary analyses and proofs so referenced items can be found. Footnotes can hold useful detail that a typical reader can skip; they should not carry a missing definition or a claim-defining assumption.

## Conclusion and implications

Return to the economic answer and verified contribution without copying the abstract sentence for sentence. Include implications only within the evidence or model's scope. A welfare or policy conclusion requires the outcomes, costs, assumptions, and affected population that support it.

Include a concrete unresolved question or extension when it follows from this study and serves the requested conclusion. Preserve material limitations and claim-defining qualifications; do not create a fixed number of limitations or future-research paragraphs. Apply the existing [confident-prose contract](../../shared-references/business-confident-prose.md) with the user's instructions taking precedence.

## Revision and submission-facing checks

Check whether the title, abstract, introduction, main result, and conclusion describe the same question, sample, and claim. Trace numerical statements to their exhibits, and citations to their verified sources. Review reader comprehension and author voice across coauthored sections.

When submission preparation is requested, verify the current outlet's replication, data-access, citation, formatting, and AI-use requirements from its official guidance. The upstream collection's policy summaries are not a current rulebook. Use the existing results, source-audit, prereview, rebuttal, and presentation skills for those requested deliverables.

Completion means the requested draft, section, or revision has this selected framework applied with factual and logical fidelity. Do not create additional reports or launch separate reviewers solely because the framework is available.
