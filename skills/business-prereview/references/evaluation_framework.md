# Master Thesis Evaluation Framework

## Overview

Use this rubric for master's thesis review only.

Evaluation dimensions:

1. Theoretical Contribution
2. Methodological Rigor
3. Data And Result Credibility
4. Writing And Academic Compliance

Default weights:

- Theoretical Contribution: 30%
- Methodological Rigor: 30%
- Data And Result Credibility: 25%
- Writing And Academic Compliance: 15%

If the school provides official weights, override defaults and document the override.

## Dimension 1: Theoretical Contribution (30%)

### What To Check

- Research problem clarity and practical/academic significance.
- Gap statement quality (what is unknown and why this thesis matters).
- Contribution specificity (not generic claims).
- Alignment between contribution claim and actual findings.

### Score Anchors

- `5` Excellent: contribution is explicit, novel enough for master's level, and evidenced throughout.
- `4` Good: clear contribution with minor over-claim or framing gaps.
- `3` Acceptable: value exists but contribution statement is weak or generic.
- `2` Weak: contribution is unclear, duplicated, or unsupported.
- `1` Poor: no identifiable contribution.

### Common Red Flags

- "Hot topic" claim without recent literature support.
- Gap statement is descriptive only (no critical synthesis).
- Thesis objective drifts across chapters.

## Dimension 2: Methodological Rigor (30%)

### What To Check

- Method-choice fit with research question.
- Design transparency (sampling, variable definition, coding protocol, interview protocol, model setup).
- Validity controls (robustness checks, alternative explanations, triangulation).
- Logical consistency from theory to method to interpretation.

### Score Anchors

- `5` Excellent: method is well-justified, reproducible, and limitations are explicitly handled.
- `4` Good: method mostly sound with minor design/reporting gaps.
- `3` Acceptable: method is usable but important controls/details are missing.
- `2` Weak: major fit or execution issues; evidence chain is fragile.
- `1` Poor: method is inappropriate or cannot support claims.

### Common Red Flags

- Method labels are inaccurate (for example, calling literature summary a full method).
- Theory section and analysis section are disconnected.
- No explanation for sample/comparison group selection.

## Dimension 3: Data And Result Credibility (25%)

### What To Check

- Data traceability (source, period, cleaning rules, exclusions).
- Table/figure integrity (readability, labels, source notes, consistency with text).
- Result stability (sensitivity checks or counterfactual reasoning as applicable).
- Claim-evidence proportionality.

### Score Anchors

- `5` Excellent: data and findings are fully traceable, internally consistent, and credible.
- `4` Good: findings are convincing with minor traceability issues.
- `3` Acceptable: core findings hold but data documentation is incomplete.
- `2` Weak: substantial traceability or consistency problems.
- `1` Poor: findings are not verifiable.

### Common Red Flags

- Numeric statements without source citation.
- Figure/table style inconsistency or unclear screenshots.
- Contradictions between hypotheses, tables, and interpretation text.

## Dimension 4: Writing And Academic Compliance (15%)

### What To Check

- Academic writing quality (clarity, precision, paragraph logic, chapter flow).
- Citation and reference consistency (in-text vs bibliography, format compliance).
- Terminology and abbreviation rules (first-use definition, correct translation).
- Program formatting requirements (abstract, headings, page numbering, appendix metadata).

### Score Anchors

- `5` Excellent: writing is clear and professional; formatting and citations are fully compliant.
- `4` Good: mostly compliant with minor language or formatting defects.
- `3` Acceptable: understandable but repeated compliance issues remain.
- `2` Weak: frequent writing and citation problems reduce credibility.
- `1` Poor: severe writing/compliance defects block formal acceptance.

### Common Red Flags

- In-text citations missing from reference list.
- Reference metadata incomplete or inconsistent.
- Undefined abbreviations and inconsistent term usage.

## Method-Specific Add-Ons

### Quantitative Master's Thesis

Extra checks:

- Identification logic and assumptions are stated.
- Fixed effects/controls and robustness checks are justified.
- Variable construction and hypothesis mapping are consistent.

### Qualitative Master's Thesis

Extra checks:

- Case selection logic is explicit.
- Coding and theme generation are auditable.
- Triangulation and negative-case handling are discussed.

### Interdisciplinary Master's Thesis

Extra checks:

- Cross-discipline concepts are defined without drift.
- Integration framework is explicit (not parallel unrelated sections).
- Evaluation criteria from each discipline are reconciled.

## Weighted Total And Decision Bands

Recommended conversion:

- Convert each dimension score (1-5) to weighted total on 100-point scale.
- Suggested interpretation:
  - 90-100: Excellent, recommend pass with minor edits.
  - 75-89: Good, recommend pass with targeted revisions.
  - 60-74: Borderline pass, major revisions required.
  - below 60: Not pass-ready, substantial revision before re-review.

Always state if bands are local defaults or official school policy.

## Evidence Marking Rule

When evidence is incomplete, append `EVIDENCE_GAP` to that statement.

Example:

- "Claim on innovation degree remains uncertain due to missing robustness appendix. EVIDENCE_GAP"

This prevents overconfident judgments and preserves auditability.
