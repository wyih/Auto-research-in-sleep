# Exhibits and discussant presentations

## Regression tables

Select the coefficient rows and comparison columns needed for the slide's claim. Collapse nuisance controls and fixed effects into clear Yes/No or specification lines. Retain standard errors or confidence intervals, N, clustering, and consequential sample changes. Put the full table in backup when useful.

Use column labels that explain what changes across specifications. Highlight the central estimate with restrained emphasis and give its economic interpretation in one sentence. Show rescaling arithmetic and the source of each input in notes. A reduction may simplify the display while preserving the contrast on which the conclusion depends.

## Figures and numbers

- Reuse a legible source figure or crop a panel while retaining axes, units, legend, baseline, intervals, and required notes. Rebuild an exhibit when source data/code are available and readability requires it.
- Exact numbers come from tables/data or recorded output, with conventional rounding. Describe a plot's visible shape when the plot is the only source.
- Event studies retain the reference period, treatment marker, event-time support, and confidence-interval convention. A normalized zero is identified as the reference value. Use the estimator selected by the design.
- Comparisons retain consistent scales, samples, units, and horizons. Distinguish percentage changes, percentage points, basis points, and log points.
- If the manuscript and exhibit conflict, resolve against underlying outputs when available. Otherwise report the affected number/claim as unresolved and omit an unsupported headline.

## Discussant role

Summarize the authors' question, design/model, finding, and contribution fairly, at the depth the audience needs. Spend most of the speaking allocation on a small number of consequential comments.

For each comment, show the paper's actual evidence location, the issue, its implication for the conclusion, and a concrete analysis or reframing that would resolve it. Distinguish the authors' estimates from the discussant's own calculations. Keep minor editorial notes separate from the substantive discussion. Use related literature only after verifying the cited paper and the relevant claim.

A discussant deck is complete when its consequential comments are supported and actionable within the allotted time. Sharing it or emailing the authors requires the user's authorization.
